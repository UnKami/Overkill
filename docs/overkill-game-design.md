# OVERKILL — Game Design Document v1

## 1. High Concept

A roguelike deckbuilder (Slay the Spire structure) where the core resource — **Overkill (OK)** — is generated by dealing damage in excess of what's needed to kill an enemy. OK is the game's single currency, persisting across the run and spent on card upgrades, new card purchases, and unlocking a special tier of Overkill-Gated cards. Separately, specific cards and relics can grant a hit **Spillage** — excess damage carries to the next enemy instead of banking as OK — turning "cash in now for currency" versus "spend now for board-clear tempo" into a real per-turn decision, not a universal default either way.

---

## 2. Core Resource: Overkill (OK)

- **Generation**: On any kill, `OK gained = max(0, damage dealt − enemy HP remaining before the hit)` — **unless the hit has Spillage active** (see Section 2.1), in which case that kill generates 0 OK and the full excess carries forward instead.
- **Persistence**: OK is a run-long currency, banked immediately, spent at shops / rest sites — never spent mid-combat, and never spent on the post-combat card reward (see Section 3). Combat = generation. Meta-layer = spending. This split keeps combat decisions about positioning/sequencing, and spending decisions about deck-building.
- **Visibility**: Running OK total always visible in the HUD. Each kill shows a floating "+N OK" callout so the feedback loop is legible in the moment.
- **Passive trickle (safety net)**: a small, flat amount of OK is granted independent of overkill performance — a modest per-combat baseline (regardless of how clean the kills were) and a fixed amount from treasure nodes — so a rough combat stretch or an off-archetype run never leaves a player with literally nothing to spend. This is a floor, not a meaningful income source; the bulk of OK should still come from playing well, or the core mechanic stops mattering.

### 2.1 Spillage — the optional alternative to banking OK

Certain cards, relics, and buffs grant a hit **Spillage**: rather than converting excess damage into OK, it carries forward as damage against the next enemy in the same attack's resolution (single-target Spillage cards need a "next enemy" to exist — no valid next target means the excess is simply lost, not banked as OK either, which is the real cost of choosing Spillage on a fight where it might whiff).

This creates the actual build-around tension: a Spillage-leaning deck plays for tempo and board-clear (chain-killing a pack of weak enemies in one swing) at the cost of forgoing that combat's OK income; a pure-banking deck ignores Spillage entirely and maximizes currency, accepting slower on-board clears. Advanced/rare cards can split the difference (e.g., "50% Spillage, 50% banked as OK") once the base version is proven in play — not needed for a first playable version.

**Spillage does not stack with itself across multiple kills in one AOE swing** beyond what the effect naturally does (excess from kill 1 becomes damage into enemy 2; if that also kills, its own excess carries to enemy 3, and so on) — a full Spillage cleave is a real, visible chain, not an abstracted total.

## 3. Spending OK — Three Sinks (plus one thing OK never touches)

1. **Upgrade cards** — cost scales with rarity/current upgrade level (Common < Uncommon < Rare, and each successive upgrade costs more).
2. **Acquire cards and items at shops** — shops sell cards, relics, and potions, all priced in OK (there is no second currency; see Section 3.1). This is the primary deliberate "spend" moment in the game.
3. **Unlock Overkill-Gated cards** (see Section 4) — two-step gate: prove capability, then pay.

**The post-combat card reward is never priced.** Pick freely from the offered options, same as a standard deckbuilder reward screen — OK is for deliberate shop/upgrade decisions the player chooses to make, not a tax on every reward they're handed. This matters more than it looks: pricing every pickup made the currency feel like bookkeeping rather than a resource being spent on purpose.

**Design rule**: OK should never trivially cap out. Prices should scale per act so that late-game OK generation (which will be much higher in absolute numbers) doesn't let players buy everything. Use a soft-cap curve: price = base × (1.15 ^ number_already_purchased_this_run).

### 3.1 One currency, not two

Earlier drafts of this design kept Gold as a separate currency for relics/potions. That's been dropped — OK is the only currency in the game, covering everything Gold previously did. Two currencies solving the same underlying problem (both were just "spend accumulated resource on stuff") added tracking overhead without adding a real decision; collapsing them makes OK the single thing every economic decision in the run revolves around, which is a better fit for a game whose entire identity is "your combat performance funds your progression." The passive trickle in Section 2 exists specifically to keep this single-currency model from ever fully drying up.

## 4. Overkill-Gated Cards (the hook)

A rare tier of cards (call it **"Excess" rarity**, sitting above Rare) that cannot be purchased or added to your deck until you've *proven* you can generate a qualifying single-hit OK value at least once in the current run.

- **Unlock condition**: "Requires a single kill with 25+ Overkill" (thresholds scale by act: Act 1 ≈ 15-25, Act 2 ≈ 30-45, Act 3 ≈ 50-70, Act 4/final ≈ 80-100+).
- **Once unlocked**: the card becomes purchasable with OK at shops/rewards for the rest of the run, like any other card — the threshold is a one-time key, not a repeated cost.
- **Character-specific gates**: different classes have different natural OK curves (burst vs volume), so thresholds should be tuned per class rather than universal, or gates should come in multiple flavors ("25+ OK in one hit" vs "3 kills with 10+ OK each in one turn" vs "40+ OK from a single card").
- **Why this works**: it turns "I want that card" into a concrete in-combat objective for a few fights, without forcing a linear grind — you're not spending currency to unlock it, you're proving your deck already does the thing.

**Sample Excess-tier cards:**
- *Trophy* (Attack): Deal damage equal to 150% of the OK generated by your last kill this combat. Scales with your own overkill loop.
- *Gluttony* (Power): Whenever you Overkill by 15+, gain 1 Strength for the rest of combat.
- *Reaper's Toll* (Skill): Retain. At the start of your turn, if you Overkilled last turn, draw 2 cards.
- *Zero Sum* (Attack, X-cost): Deals X×4 damage. If this kills the enemy, refund 1 energy per 10 OK generated (min 0).

## 5. Archetypes (how you *generate* OK, not just how hard you hit)

Each archetype should feel as distinct as StS's Ironclad/Silent/Defect/Watcher — same currency, wildly different playstyles.

### Burst
- Rare, huge one-shot kills. X-cost finishers, damage stacking over a turn, "all-in" cards.
- High OK spikes, unlocks Excess-tier cards early.
- Risk: if the finisher whiffs or the enemy has too much HP, you lose the whole turn's setup and take damage undefended.

### Volume
- Many small kills across multi-enemy trash fights. Cheap attacks, AoE, weak-enemy targeting priority.
- Small-but-steady OK income. Strong early economy, favors upgrade-heavy builds over flashy unlocks.
- Weak against single tough bosses where there's only one kill to get OK from.

### Amplifier / Precision
- Cards that convert existing OK generation into more OK, independent of raw damage. ("When you Overkill, gain 50% more OK this combat," "Overkill damage counts as 25% higher for OK purposes.")
- Build-agnostic multiplier layer — works on top of Burst or Volume, making it a support archetype rather than a third parallel lane. Good for players who want to combine strategies. Notably, this archetype is the natural counterpoint to a Spillage build rather than a competitor to it — Amplifier cards only benefit hits that bank as OK, so a deck leaning hard into Spillage gets little from them, which is a fine and intentional tension between the two card pools.

### Conversion / Control
- Trades resources (Block, HP, card draw, extra turns) for *guaranteed* OK on the next kill regardless of enemy HP overshoot. ("Execute: if enemy HP ≤ 5, treat this kill as if you'd dealt 20 excess damage.")
- For control/defensive decks that don't naturally overshoot — buys OK directly rather than earning it opportunistically. Should be the least efficient path (worst OK-per-energy) but the most reliable, appealing to risk-averse players.

**Cross-archetype tension the player feels every fight**: Do I overkill this specific enemy now (spend a turn chasing excess, possibly leaving myself open) or play it safe and take the "clean kill, no OK" line? Enemies with dangerous end-of-turn effects or multi-hit patterns should sometimes punish greedy overkill attempts — e.g., an enemy that gains a shield/retaliates if not killed within 2 turns, making low-precision overkill swings costly.

## 6. Enemy & Boss Design

Regular enemies (trash/elites) should have HP pools tuned so that a *reasonable* attack overshoots by a meaningful, farmable amount — this is what makes Volume viable. If HP pools are always exactly matched to typical damage, overkill becomes accidental rather than a real design space.

**Bosses are the hard design problem**: huge HP pools mean overkill is naturally easier to generate in raw numbers (any decent finisher will overshoot by a lot on a boss with 300 HP), which risks making bosses the *only* good OK source and trivializing the trash-farming loop. Solutions:
- Scale Excess-tier unlock thresholds so they can't all be trivially cleared by "hit the boss once."
- Bosses could have **HP-gated damage caps** on specific turns (shields, dodge windows, phase transitions) forcing precise burst timing rather than "just play your whole hand."
- Consider a **boss-specific OK bonus track**: bosses drop a unique relic/card only if you land the killing blow with OK *above* a boss-specific threshold — turning the final hit of a boss fight into its own mini-puzzle (do you hold back a burst card for the kill shot, risking the fight dragging on?).

**Elites** are a good middle ground: moderate HP, dangerous enough that greedy overkill attempts carry real risk, ideal testing ground for Excess-tier unlock thresholds.

## 7. Relics (equivalent layer)

Relics should modify the *rate or rules* of OK generation, not just deal more damage, to keep them additive to the deckbuilding puzzle:

- **Passive generators**: "Gain 1 OK at the start of combat automatically."
- **Conversion relics**: "Convert 5 unused Block into 1 OK at end of turn."
- **Rule-benders**: "Overkill damage against enemies below 50% max HP counts double." / "First kill each combat generates minimum 10 OK regardless of overshoot."
- **Risk/reward relics**: "Gain 50% more OK from kills, but take 1 damage at end of turn if you did not Overkill this turn." (Punishes Conversion/Control turtling, rewards commitment to the mechanic.)

Avoid pure damage-boost relics as OK-adjacent rewards — they trivialize the currency instead of interacting with it.

## 8. Meta-Progression / Run Structure

Keep the StS shape (3-4 Acts, branching map, rest sites, shops, elites, bosses):

- **Shops** sell cards, relics, and potions, all priced in OK (Section 3.1) — this makes shops the primary "spend" moment, not just a side visit.
- **Rest sites** could offer an OK-spend option alongside Rest/Upgrade: "Smelt" a card (sacrifice it) for a flat OK refund, useful for pruning bad early picks into currency.
- **Card removal** (a scarce, valuable service in StS) costs OK, tying deck-thinning directly to how well you've been playing the overkill game.
- **Difficulty scaling (Ascension-equivalent)**: higher tiers could reduce OK generation multiplier (e.g., -10% OK per hit) or raise Excess-tier thresholds, rather than just raising enemy HP/damage — keeps the added difficulty on-theme.

## 9. Anti-Degenerate-Strategy Guardrails

Things to explicitly watch for in playtesting:
- **"Always overkill, no downside" trap**: if OK has zero opportunity cost, the interesting decision collapses. Enemies with retaliation-on-overkill-attempt-failure, or a "wasted overkill" penalty against enemies immune to excess (see below), keep tension alive.
- **Immune/Armored enemy type**: an enemy or status ("Tempered") where damage above HP is capped/lost entirely for OK purposes — forces players to occasionally take the "clean kill" line and rewards precision, not just brute force. Good design lever for late-game difficulty.
- **OK inflation**: if late-run OK numbers get too large, the currency stops feeling meaningful (like Gold does in StS by Act 3). The escalating price curve (Section 3) and per-act threshold scaling (Section 4) are the main levers to keep it tight.
